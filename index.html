How AI Video Repair Works

AI Video Repair applies up to four fixes in one pass: video super-resolution to rebuild detail, denoising to cut grain, frame interpolation to smooth choppy motion, and lighting correction for flat or backlit scenes. Only the fixes your footage actually needs run by default.

Enhance: Real Detail, Not Just a Sharper Blur

Video super-resolution rebuilds missing texture instead of just sharpening edges. It turns on automatically at 1080p or below, stays off above that, and disables at 4K since there's nothing left to add. Best for soft phone or downloaded clips; skip it on pixelated screenshots, no tool can invent missing detail.

AI Video Repair: Every Fix, Automated

Traditional editing software leaves you manually adjusting sharpen and noise-reduction filters and exposure correction — sharpening just amplifies existing edges instead of rebuilding detail, and balancing noise by eye takes real time. AI Video Repair automates all of it in one pass, no manual tuning required.

Video Denoise: Cut the Grain Without Losing Texture

Removes grain from low-light or high-ISO footage. Off by default since not every clip needs it, and over-denoising can soften texture. Best for night or old camcorder footage; skip it on clean daylight shots.

Frame Interpolation: Smoother Motion at the Right Frame Rate

Generates in-between frames to smooth choppy motion. Auto-enabled at 16fps or below, off between 16 and 60fps, disabled above 60fps or when fps can't be read. Best for old slideshow-style clips; skip it on fast action already shot at high frame rate, it can warp moving edges.

AI Lighting: Rescue Underexposed or Flat Footage

An Off/Medium/High slider brightens backlit or flat footage without blowing out highlights. Best for dim interior or backlit clips; avoid High on footage that's already well-lit, it can wash out contrast.
